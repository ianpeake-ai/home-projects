What We're Bringing Home.

Seven stops. Three countries. One dog. Everything worth putting in the car, and why it's worth it.

Days 2 & 3 · Netherlands

Haarlem.
What Amsterdam thinks it is.

Two full days. Grote Markt, Jopenkerk, Spaarne river. The boot starts filling here.

Food & Pantry
Aged Gouda / brokkelkaas
€6–10
The real thing: two-year-old, crystalline, closer to aged parmesan than anything you'd recognise as supermarket Gouda. Ask for extra belegen (1yr+) or oud (2yr+). Ask them to vacuum-seal it. It will improve dinner at South Acre every time you cut a slice, for weeks.
→ Kaashuis Tromp or similar kaaswinkel on or near Grote Markt. Not a market stall.
Komijnekaas / cumin Gouda
€4–6
The cumin-seeded version. A Dutch staple that barely exists in UK shops. Same shop, same ask, takes up no extra space.
→ Same kaaswinkel as above.
Appelstroop
€3
Dark, thick, reduced apple syrup — not jam, not sauce. A Dutch breakfast condiment that also does extraordinary things to gravies and cheese on toast. Keeps indefinitely. This is the one everyone forgets to buy and then can't find anywhere in the UK.
→ Any Albert Heijn or deli.
Indonesian pantry / sambal & kecap manis
€2–4 each
The Dutch colonial relationship with Indonesia means their supermarkets carry condiments that UK shops approximate but never match. Sambal oelek, sambal badjak, kecap manis (sweet soy sauce). The Conimex brand is the workhorse. Building blocks of a nasi goreng that actually tastes right.
→ Albert Heijn — the Indonesian aisle is the whole point.
Drop / zoute, salty liquorice
€2–3
Get the salty variety. Dubbel zout is the extreme version. Genuinely divisive. Worth having the argument.
→ Any supermarket or sweet shop.
Hagelslag / chocolate sprinkles
€2
Sounds absurd. Works on buttered bread for breakfast. Takes up no room. Buy a box.
→ Albert Heijn.
Boterbabbelaars
€2–4
Hard butter-caramel sweets, individually wrapped. The name means something like "butter chatterers." Intensely buttery, slow-dissolving, nothing complex — just the platonic ideal of a butter sweet. The kind of thing you buy as a car snack and think about six months later. Get a bag for the drive to Zierikzee.
→ Any supermarket, sweet shop, or market stall.
Drink
Jopenkerk bottled beer
€12–15
Buy while you're there for a drink. Their Koyt is a medieval-recipe grain ale — unusual, not widely exported, genuinely interesting. Mixed six: Koyt, Mooie Nel IPA, and whatever seasonal they have. Buy more than you think you need.
→ Jopenkerk brewery shop, directly.
Jenever / oude
€18–25
Amber, malty, nothing like London gin — closer to a light whisky. Zuidam or Van Wees are reliable producers. Drink it neat, slightly chilled, small glass. Survives the whole trip in the boot without complaint.
→ A good slijterij (off-licence) or deli near the Grote Markt.
Art, Craft & Design
Ceramics & studio pottery
varies
The Dutch have a long ceramic tradition. Not Delftware tourist stuff — contemporary Dutch studio pottery. Worth looking in windows as you walk the Kleine Houtstraat and side streets. No specific shop to name; let the windows do the work.
→ Kleine Houtstraat and surrounding streets.
Vintage & antique shops
whatever they ask
Dutch households historically had excellent taste in domestic objects. Weights, measures, old glass, linens. The cluster around Smedestraat is worth a slow look.
→ Smedestraat and side streets off the centre.
Flower bulbs take home
€5–12
Tulips, alliums, narcissi. You're in the right place at the right time. Legal to import to the UK from the EU for personal use. A bag of good allium or late-season tulip bulbs from a proper bulb seller costs almost nothing and pays off next spring at South Acre. Not from a tourist stand — find a proper bulb shop.
→ Bulb market or specialist shop, Haarlem centre.
Pharmacy
Diclofenac gel / Voltaren 2%
€8–12
Full-strength 2% gel, large tube, no prescription needed. The UK rations you to smaller packs at lower strength unless you have a script. Buy two tubes here and you're sorted for the year.
→ Any apotheek (pharmacy). Staff speak English, no awkwardness.
Arnica gel
€4–6
OTC everywhere, cheap, good. For bruises, sprains, and the long walking days you're about to have.
→ Any apotheek or drogist.

Day 4 · Zeeland, Netherlands

Zierikzee.
Harbour lunch. Zeeland fills the cool box.

A lunch stop, not a shopping destination. But Zeeland has its own pantry and it's worth knowing.

Eat here · don't take home
Zeeuws bolus eat now
€1.50–2 each
A local pastry — sticky, dark, cinnamon-caramelised spiral. Doesn't travel well. This is a consume-immediately purchase: harbour terrace, coffee, one each. Get two.
→ Any bakery in Zierikzee.
Food & Pantry
Zeeuwse mosterd / Zeeland mustard
€3–4
Coarser than Dijon, grainy, made for shellfish and aged cheese. Different enough from what you already have to be worth the boot space.
→ Local deli or supermarket.
Zeeuws spek cool box
€4–6
Cured, lightly spiced pork belly — somewhere between streaky bacon and lardo. Sold vacuum-packed. Sliced thin on good bread, it's a revelation. Into the cool box from here onwards.
→ Local butcher or deli in Zierikzee.
Drink
Emelisse or Kees beer
€2.50–4 each
Zeeland's best craft brewers. Emelisse (Kamperland) does exceptional barley wine and smoked porter. Kees (Middelburg) does excellent IPAs. Not widely exported — if you see them, buy a few bottles.
→ Local slijterij or well-stocked supermarket.

Days 4 & 5 · Belgium

Ghent.
Not Bruges. A city.

The strongest stop on the trip for food, drink, design and craft. Two days. Use them.

Food & Pantry
Tierenteyn mustard non-negotiable
€4–6
Sharp, hot in a horseradish direction, made without vinegar since 1790. They ladle it into a small ceramic pot from a stone jar behind a roller shutter. Nothing like Dijon — a different condiment entirely. If you come home without this, you've made a mistake.
→ Tierenteyn-Verlent, Groentenmarkt 3. That's the address.
Cuberdons / neuzekes eat within 3 weeks
€6–8
Raspberry-flavoured gumdrop cones — Ghent's own confection, barely known outside the city. Short shelf life, so buy them on Day 5 rather than Day 4 morning. Keep them cool. They'll make it home easily if you do.
→ Temmerman (Kraanlei 79) or Verdonck. Not the tourist-facing stalls.
Ganda ham cool box
€8–12
Ghent's own dry-cured ham, aged 10+ months, mild and nutty. Vacuum-packed slices. Find it at the Groot Vleeshuis — the old meat hall on the Groentenmarkt, now a showcase for Flemish charcuterie. Dog-friendly outside.
→ Groot Vleeshuis, Groentenmarkt.
Speculoos / proper baked
€3–5
Not the Lotus spread (though that's fine too) — a block of proper baked speculoos from a good bakery. Dense, deeply spiced, keeps for weeks.
→ Independent bakery, Patershol area or central Ghent.
Belgian chocolate
€12–18
Skip anything near the tourist drag. A small box of pralines from an independent chocolatier. They survive two days in a coolish bag if you keep the car out of direct sun.
→ Small independent chocolatier, Patershol area.
Drink
Trappist & lambic mixed six
€25–35
Build a mixed six from a specialist beer shop: Westmalle Dubbel, Orval, Achel, and a bottle of Tilquin Gueuze — a modern gueuze blender making some of the best examples available. The Tilquin is genuinely special: sour, complex, long. About €7–9 for the 75cl bottle alone.
→ Specialist beer shop, Patershol / centre. Not a supermarket.
Gruut beer
€3–4 per bottle
Brewed with herbs instead of hops — a genuinely medieval recipe revived in Ghent. The most locally rooted beer on the whole list. Buy a few bottles to take home.
→ Gruut brewery shop or good off-licence.
Duvel tulip glasses
€3–5 each
Buy these. They are tools, not ornaments. Belgian beer poured into the correct glass is a measurably different experience. Wrap them in a jumper in the boot.
→ Any good Belgian beer shop.
Art, Craft & Design
Vrijdagmarkt area / design & homeware
varies
Ghent has a serious local design scene — independent homeware, fashion, ceramics. The shops around the Vrijdagmarkt aren't trying to be Amsterdam or Antwerp. They're doing their own quieter, more interesting thing. Walk slowly with no agenda.
→ Vrijdagmarkt and surrounding streets.
Superette / design & objects
varies
A well-regarded Ghent design and lifestyle shop. Small objects, printed matter, ceramics. Worth a look if you're passing.
→ Search current address before going — it moves.
Belgian linen
€40–120
Ghent was historically a linen city — the word "lawn" as in fabric derives from the nearby region. Belgian linen is genuinely world-class. A good tablecloth or set of napkins is an object that lasts thirty years. You won't find it cheap, but the price per year of use is almost nothing.
→ Specialist linen or homeware shop in central Ghent.
Books
€15–30
Ghent has excellent independent bookshops. Art books, design books, photography books travel well and are often cheaper than UK equivalents. Even if you don't read Dutch or French, the image-led stuff is universal.
→ Independent bookshops, Ghent centre.
Pharmacy
Diclofenac / Arnica if not bought in Haarlem
same as NL
Belgian pharmacies are equally well-stocked and equally unfussy. Same items, same ask, same result.
→ Any pharmacie / apotheek.

Day 6 · Northern France

Clairmarais & Saint-Omer.
Marshes. Pungent cheese. A distillery detour.

Primarily a nature stop, but northern France has a serious pantry if you know where to look.

Drink
Genièvre / Distillerie Persyn, Houlle
€20–28
The distillery is in Houlle — about 10km west of Saint-Omer, a short detour. Their genièvre vieille is barrel-aged, juniper-forward, and genuinely excellent. This is the one spirit on the trip worth paying attention to. If you don't detour, ask for it at any good cave in Saint-Omer — it's local enough to be stocked.
→ Distillerie Persyn, Houlle — or any good wine shop in Saint-Omer.
Food & Pantry
Maroilles cheese triple-bag it
€6–10
The definitive northern French cheese. Square, washed rind, orange, intensely pungent when ripe. Wrap it thoroughly — triple-bag, coolest part of the boot. Worth every complicated minute of the journey home. Also look for Boulette d'Avesnes if you see it: cone-shaped, alarmingly orange, equally pungent, even rarer.
→ Fromagerie or Saturday market in Saint-Omer.
Chicorée Leroux
€2–3
Liquid chicory coffee additive — a Nord staple, barely known south of Paris. Adds a roasted, slightly bitter depth to coffee or cake batter. Small, keeps forever, as regional as it gets.
→ Any supermarket in the region.
Vergeoise sugar
€2 a bag
Dark or blonde beet sugar with a fudgy, damp texture. Essential for proper speculaas, tarte au sucre, or any baking that benefits from a deeper caramel note. Get two bags — they weigh nothing.
→ Any supermarket.
Local honey
€6–8
The Audomarois marshes produce good honey from varied coastal and marsh flora. If you pass a producteur sign by the road or a market stall, stop.
→ Market stall or roadside producer.
Brocante finds
whatever they ask
Not a design stop, but northern French brocantes (secondhand dealers) sometimes have excellent domestic objects at low prices — old weights and measures, enamelware, linen, glass. If you pass one with things outside, stop. Don't go looking, but don't drive past either.
→ Roadside brocante, wherever you find one.

Day 6 evening & Day 7 morning · Hauts-de-France

Montreuil-sur-Mer.
Ramparts. Champagne. A Picardy cake.

A walled town that exists for its own reasons. One night at the Château. Morning walk before Calais.

Food & Pantry
Gâteau battu eat within 3 days
€6–9
A tall, brioche-like Picardy cake — golden, eggy, slightly sweet. Superb for breakfast on the last morning at the Château with butter and jam. Doesn't keep beyond the trip but doesn't need to. Buy it on the evening of Day 6 or morning of Day 7.
→ Good boulangerie in Montreuil town.
Local honey & preserves
€5–8
Coastal herb honey, fruit jellies from what grows nearby. Market or farm shop if you find one near the ramparts.
→ Market stall or small producer, Montreuil.
Drink
Calvados / apple brandy
€18–25
You're close enough to Normandy for apple spirit culture to be present. A decent Calvados VS from a good cave. Gets opened on a cold October night and justifies itself immediately.
→ Any good cave or wine merchant in Montreuil.
Grower Champagne
€18–30
You're in northern France; this is the time. Not Moët, not a supermarket brand — look for RM (récoltant-manipulant) on the label, which means a small grower making their own wine. Better than most famous houses and substantially cheaper than UK retail. Buy two: one for home, one for Pete's mum in Walton-on-Trent.
→ Wine merchant or cave, Montreuil.
Art & Craft
Montreuil studios & galleries
varies
The town has quietly attracted artists and makers. Small walled place, slightly off the map, old buildings with low rents — the conditions that produce studios. A slow walk inside the ramparts on Day 7 morning before leaving for Calais. Something unexpected in a doorway is more likely here than almost anywhere else on the trip.
→ Inside the walled town, morning of Day 7.

Day 7 · Before the tunnel

Cité Europe, Calais.
Move fast. Buy volume. Leave.

This is a loading operation, not a shopping experience. One person in Carrefour, one outside with Echo. Thirty minutes maximum. Everything below is in the hypermarket.

Pantry & Fridge
Butter / beurre de baratte demi-sel
€2–3 per 250g
Paysan Breton or Président. Visible salt crystals. Proper fat content. Not like UK salted butter — a different object. Freezes perfectly. Buy 8–10 packs and freeze half when you get home. This is the single best-value purchase of the Carrefour run.
→ Dairy aisle, Carrefour.
Duck fat / graisse de canard
€4–6
Large tin, Rougié or similar. Cheap, lasts months in the fridge, transforms roast potatoes entirely. This was the collective miss of all the models asked about this trip. It should be near the top of your list.
→ Tinned goods aisle, Carrefour.
Comté / 12 or 18 month
€6–9
Vacuum-packed wedge. The 18-month has the same crystalline depth as the aged Gouda from Haarlem — nutty, long, complex. UK price for equivalent quality is roughly double.
→ Cheese section, Carrefour.
Crème de marrons / Clément Faugier
€3–4 each
Big tins of sweetened chestnut purée. On crêpes, on yoghurt, on a spoon at midnight. Get two tins.
→ Jam and preserves aisle.
Cornichons / Maille or Amora, big jar
€3–4
The tiny crispy ones. Sharper and crunchier than anything UK supermarkets sell. One jar won't be enough.
→ Condiments aisle.
Lentilles du Puy / Sabarot
€3–4 a bag
The grey-green lentils in UK supermarkets are a different vegetable in every way that matters. Get two bags. Goes with the confit de canard. There's your October dinner.
→ Dried goods aisle.
Tinned cassoulet / La Belle Chaurienne
€5–7
Dinner for two on a November night when you can't face cooking. Non-negotiable.
→ Tinned goods.
Confit de canard / tinned, La Belle Chaurienne
€6–8
Two legs in a tin. Goes on a bed of Puy lentils with Tierenteyn mustard on the side. That's October sorted.
→ Tinned goods.
Tinned sardines / Mouettes d'Arvor
€3–5 per tin
In butter or olive oil. Buy six. The UK tinned sardine market is sad by comparison.
→ Fish and tinned goods aisle.
Dijon mustard / Carrefour own label
€2–3
Get two large jars even if you have Tierenteyn — they're different things for different uses. Carrefour own label is genuinely good.
→ Condiments aisle.
Espelette pepper / piment d'Espelette AOC
€5–7
Basque AOC chilli: mild heat, distinctive flavour, used throughout French cooking. Barely available in the UK; expensive when you find it. Small jar, takes up no room.
→ Spice aisle.
Walnut oil
€5–8
Good French walnut oil transforms a green salad into something worth eating. UK versions are either expensive or thin. Huilerie Leblanc if they stock it; own-brand otherwise.
→ Oils aisle.
Fleur de sel & herbes de Provence
€2–4 each
Essentially free at French supermarket prices. Used constantly. Buy them now rather than paying £6 each at a deli in Glasgow.
→ Spice and seasoning aisle.
French flour / T55 & T45
€1.50–2 a bag
If you bake: T55 is all-purpose, T45 is for pastry and brioche. Makes a noticeable difference. A bag of each, flat in the boot.
→ Baking aisle.
Jambon crisps / Bret's or Lay's Saveur Jambon
€1.50–2 per bag
Get a stack. You know this already.
→ Snacks aisle.
Financiers & madeleines / bulk bags
€3–4
Cheap, good, won't survive long after opening. That's fine.
→ Biscuits aisle.
Wine & Spirits
Wine / a full case minimum
€7–14 per bottle
Skip the famous regions — priced up for British tourists. Go Loire whites (Muscadet, Menetou-Salon), red Chinon or Saumur-Champigny, Côtes du Rhône for everyday. A case of twelve at €85–140 will cost you £180–250 in the UK. You're allowed 18 litres per person — that's 48 bottles between two. You're not going to get close.
→ Wine section, Carrefour — worth an unhurried fifteen minutes.
Pharmacy
Biafine don't miss this
€6–8
A French emulsion for minor burns, sunburn, superficial wounds and skin healing. OTC in France, basically unavailable in the UK. Use it on any sunburn within an hour and the difference is dramatic. Cult status among everyone who's discovered it. Buy two 93g tubes.
→ Pharmacie at Cité Europe or within Carrefour pharmacy section.
Spasfon / phloroglucinol
€5–7
French antispasmodic for gut cramps, IBS-type pain. OTC in France. Prescription-only in the UK. Genuinely effective for visceral spasm. Worth having in the medicine cabinet.
→ Pharmacie.
Smecta / diosmectite
€6–8
Clay-based gut settler. Better than Imodium for a lot of situations — gentler, safe for all ages. OTC in France and Belgium.
→ Pharmacie.
Doliprane / paracetamol 1000mg
€3–4
The French standard dose is a full gram. Cheap in bulk packs. Stock up.
→ Pharmacie or Carrefour health aisle.
Homéoplasmine
€5–7
Small tin, dense occlusive balm. Chapped lips, cracked skin, minor cuts, cold sore borders. Gets used constantly once you have one. You already have one and know this is true. Buy two more as spares.
→ Pharmacie.
La Roche-Posay Anthelios SPF50
€14–18
The same large tube costs £28–35 in the UK. French dermocosmetic sun cream is significantly better than UK supermarket equivalents — lighter formula, higher actual protection. Buy two.
→ Pharmacie.
Tick repellent / DEET-based
€8–12
You've been walking Zeeland marshes, Clairmarais, Montreuil ramparts. French pharmacies stock better-formulated DEET repellents than UK supermarkets. Worth having for Echo too — Labradors in long grass are tick magnets.
→ Pharmacie.
Voltaren / Diclofenac / last chance
€8–12
If you haven't already bought it in Haarlem or Ghent, do it now. Large tube, full strength.
→ Pharmacie.

The boot, honestly assessed.

Everything on this list comes to roughly €350–500 across the whole trip, of which €150–200 is the Cité Europe run. The wine alone at UK equivalents would cost twice what you paid. The Tierenteyn mustard will still be in the fridge in December. The confit de canard will rescue a grey Tuesday in October. The butter will make everything better from here until it runs out.

Echo will not care about any of it. She will, however, have had the best week of her life.
