Netherlands · Haarlem · Zierikzee · IJmuiden

Dutch.
They'll switch to English instantly. Open in Dutch anyway.

Almost everyone under 60 in the Netherlands speaks excellent English. The point of trying Dutch isn't communication — it's acknowledgement. A sentence or two in Dutch, then let them switch if they want. They will.

Dutch pronunciation notes
  • g / ch guttural throat sound, like clearing your throat softly — "gh"
  • ij / ei sounds like "eye" — rhymes with "my"
  • oe sounds like "oo" in "moon"
  • ui roughly "ow" but rounder — like "uh-ee" run together
  • eu like the French "eu" — round lips, say "uh"
  • r slightly rolled or guttural depending on region
  • v often sounds more like "f" in Dutch

Belgium · Ghent · Patershol · Graslei

Ghent.
Dutch first. Always Dutch first.

Ghent is a Flemish city. The language is Dutch. Most people under 50 speak excellent English. French is understood but slightly grates — a bit like speaking English to a Welsh speaker in Cardiff. Open in Dutch, let them switch if they want, and you're golden.

The language rule for Ghent

Lead with Dutch. Even broken Dutch, even just Goedemiddag and a smile. Ghentenaars appreciate it. If you open in French, you'll get served fine but you've placed yourself as someone who didn't bother to look up where they were. If you open in English directly, that's also fine — but Dutch first is better. They will switch to English themselves within one exchange, and that switch is a gift they're choosing to give you.

At the Tierenteyn shop
Asking for the mustard
Een potje mosterd, alstublieft
eyn POT-yuh MOS-tert, al-stuh-BLEEFT
A small pot of mustard, please
The staff at Tierenteyn are used to people who've come specifically for the mustard. Just point at the jar if needed. They'll ladle it for you.
Asking for a cold pack
Heeft u een koudepakket?
heyft ew un KOW-duh-pak-et
Do you have a cold pack? (for the journey)
Beer shop / Trappist hunting
Asking about Trappist beers
Heeft u Tilquin gueuze?
heyft ew TIL-kwan GHER-zuh
Do you have Tilquin gueuze?
Gueuze is pronounced with that soft Belgian gh — roughly "GHER-zuh." Don't say "gwee-zuh." The person behind the counter will wince.

France · Saint-Omer · Montreuil · Calais

French.
The effort is the point.

Northern French locals are not the Paris stereotype. They're generally warm to people who make an attempt. You won't be fluent — that's not the goal. The goal is the first sentence in French before they switch. They usually will.

French pronunciation notes
  • r guttural — from the back of the throat
  • u round your lips and say "ee" — no English equivalent, write as "ew"
  • eu like "uh" with rounded lips — as in peur
  • on / an / en nasal vowels — don't pronounce the n, nasalise the vowel
  • silent endings final consonants usually silent: vous = "voo", est = "ay"
  • liaison when a word ends in consonant + next starts with vowel, run them together
Basics
The essential opener — always say this first
Bonjour / Bonsoir
bon-ZHOOR / bon-SWAHR
Good day (morning/afternoon) / Good evening
In France, not saying Bonjour before anything else is mildly rude. Walk into any shop, any pharmacy, any market stall — say Bonjour first. Always. It takes one second and changes everything.
Please / Thank you / You're welcome
S'il vous plaît / Merci / De rien
seel voo PLAY / mair-SEE / duh ree-AN
Please / Thank you / You're welcome
Excuse me / Sorry
Excusez-moi / Pardon
ex-kew-zay MWAH / par-DON
Excuse me / Sorry — both very useful
Do you speak English? — said apologetically
Parlez-vous anglais? Mon français est très limité.
par-lay voo on-GLAY / mon fron-SAY ay tray lee-mee-TAY
Do you speak English? My French is very limited.
The second sentence is the difference between a tourist demand and a genuine apology. Use it. Northern French people respond well to this.
Goodbye
Au revoir / Bonne journée
oh ruh-VWAHR / bon zhoor-NAY
Goodbye / Have a good day
Bonne journée on leaving a shop is warm and normal. They'll say it back. Small exchange, good feeling.

Driving & Petrol
At a petrol station
Le plein, s'il vous plaît / Sans plomb
luh PLAN, seel voo PLAY / son PLON
Fill it up, please / Unleaded
The Hyundai takes unleaded — sans plomb (literally "without lead"). Most French forecourts are self-service now so you may not need to say anything.
Asking for directions
Excusez-moi — où est…?
ex-kew-zay MWAH — oo ay
Excuse me — where is…?
You have GPS. But if you need it: à gauche (ah GOHSH) = left, à droite (ah DRWAHT) = right, tout droit (too DRWAH) = straight ahead.

All three countries

Echo.
For when she's the conversation.

Echo will attract attention everywhere. People will ask about her, want to greet her, comment on her. Having a few phrases ready makes these exchanges warmer and faster. She won't mind either way.

Dutch · Echo phrases
Someone asks if they can pet her
Ja, ze is vriendelijk / Ze heet Echo
yah, zuh is FREEN-duh-luk / zuh heyt EH-koh
Yes, she's friendly / Her name is Echo
Asking if dogs are allowed
Zijn honden welkom?
zayn HON-dun VEL-kom
Are dogs welcome? — the single most useful phrase on the trip
Asking if there's water for her
Heeft u water voor mijn hond?
heyft ew VAH-ter for mayn HOND
Do you have water for my dog?
Dog-friendly Dutch and Belgian terraces often have bowls already. But asking this question tends to make staff bring biscuits as well.
If someone's nervous around her
Ze doet geen kwaad
zuh doot gheyn kwaht
She won't hurt anyone — literally "she does no harm"
French · Echo phrases
Asking if dogs are allowed
Les chiens sont acceptés ici?
lay shee-AN son ak-sep-TAY ee-SEE
Are dogs accepted here?
Someone admires her
Elle s'appelle Echo. Elle est très sage.
el sah-PEL EH-koh. el ay tray SAZH
She's called Echo. She's very well-behaved.
Sage (SAZH) = well-behaved, wise, calm. The best compliment a French person gives a dog. Use it proactively and they'll believe you.
Asking for water for her
De l'eau pour mon chien, s'il vous plaît?
duh LOH poor mon shee-AN, seel voo PLAY
Some water for my dog, please?
If someone's nervous or asks you to keep her away

pah duh pro-BLEM — zhuh lah tee-AN
No problem — I'll hold her.
At the vet — Haarlem · 10 June
Arriving at Dierenkliniek Kenaupark
Ik heb een afspraak voor mijn hond.
ik hep eyn AF-spraak for mayn hond
I have an appointment for my dog.
The vet in Haarlem will certainly speak English. But starting in Dutch is a good instinct. Say you're there for the tapeworm treatment: de ontwormingspil (duh ont-VOR-mings-pil) — the deworming tablet.
Asking for the certificate
Heeft u een bewijs voor de grensovergang?
heyft ew eyn buh-VAYS for duh GRENS-oh-fer-ghang
Do you have a certificate for the border crossing?
They'll know exactly what you need and will provide it. The tapeworm treatment must be documented for UK re-entry — they deal with this every day.
