Saturday 9th May 2026
Pre-Split Inspection
Confirm readiness · check for queen cells · mark the queen
⚡
If You Find Capped Queen Cells Today
Do not wait until Thursday. Split immediately. A colony at this strength can swarm before May 14th if the weather opens and capped cells are already present. Adapt the rest of this plan from today's date.
Actions
  • Suit on, smoker lit. Wait for above 14°C, dry, calm conditions
  • Full inspection — work methodically frame by frame, both sides of each frame
  • Count active brood frames — 10+ needed before proceeding to split
  • Check every frame for queen cells. Polished cups only: note and proceed. Charged cups with larvae: proceed, accelerate. Capped cells: split today.
  • Find and mark the queen if not already marked. 2026 = white. A marked queen makes Thursday's split far less error-prone. Dab on thorax only, allow to dry before replacing frame.
  • Look for polished cells in the centre of the brood nest — a positive sign of queen activity and readiness to lay
  • Assess stores on each frame — note which frames have capped honey for the split allocation
  • Write up the inspection record before removing the suit

Thursday 14th May 2026
Split Day
Move Sir David right first · Carol Klein takes original site · divide the colony 55/45
📍
The Logic of Position
Foragers navigate by GPS coordinates, not by box colour or appearance. All returning bees fly back to the original site. Carol Klein sits there waiting and gets a full foraging workforce from day one — exactly what a queenless colony needs during her most vulnerable period. Sir David moves with the queen and reorientates to his new position via the grass plug over 24–48 hours.
Before you open anything
  • Carol Klein Hive already assembled and positioned on the original site before you start — floor, brood box, feeder with cooled 1:1 syrup, roof. Do not move it once split begins.
  • Sir David's new stand ready 50cm to the right, stable and level
  • Both hives have distinct visual markers — different shape and colour, painted and dry well before today
  • Fresh grass to hand for Sir David's entrance plug
  • Suit on, smoker lit and producing cool white smoke before you approach
  • Roles agreed before opening: Ian on frames, Pete on records and equipment
The split sequence — follow this order exactly
  • Step 1 — Move Sir David's box first. Lift the entire assembled Sir David hive (floor, brood box, roof together) to his new stand 50cm right. Do this before opening anything — Carol Klein's empty box is already on the original site waiting.
  • Step 2 — Plug Sir David's entrance loosely with fresh grass immediately. Forces his bees to push out slowly over 24–48 hours and reorient to the new position rather than returning to the original site.
  • Step 3 — Open Sir David. Find the marked queen. Note which frame she is on. Do not remove her.
  • Step 4 — Build the split. Remove ~5 frames from Sir David for Carol Klein: brood in all stages (eggs AND young larvae essential), stores, pollen. Sir David keeps ~6–7 frames including the queen's frame. Approximately 55/45.
  • Step 5 — Shake extra nurse bees from Sir David frames into Carol Klein before sealing. Compensates for the nurse bees she'll lose to drift in the first 24 hours.
  • Step 6 — Seal Carol Klein. Dummy board tight against last frame. Entrance on smallest setting — open, no plug. Roof on.
  • Step 7 — Close Sir David. Entrance reduced to minimum behind the grass plug. Roof on. Hive strap on. Walk away.
  • Angle entrances very slightly differently — even a few degrees helps long-term drift reduction
  • Write up the split record — frames moved, queen confirmed, time, weather, any queen cells seen
🚫
Do Not Feed Carol Klein Today
With a May flow on, feeding the queenless split risks triggering robbing from Sir David. Carol Klein has stores on her frames. Leave her until after queen introduction on Saturday.

Saturday 16th May 2026
Destroy Cells · Introduce Queen
48hrs queenless · destroy every cell · cage introduction · expose candy immediately
⚡
The Most Important Step in the Entire Plan
Every single queen cell in Carol Klein must be found and destroyed before the queen enters the hive. One missed cell means a virgin queen will emerge and kill the introduced queen the moment she is released. Shake bees off every frame. See the wax surface clearly. Check every frame. Then check again.
Destroying queen cells — Carol Klein only
  • Suit on, smoker lit — use smoke lightly. Queenless colonies can be defensive; heavy smoke causes confusion.
  • Open Carol Klein Hive. Work methodically frame by frame.
  • Shake bees off each frame — you must see the wax surface, not through a layer of bees
  • Destroy every queen cell without exception — open cups with larvae, capped cells, any peanut-shaped structure on frame bottoms or sides. Use hive tool or finger. Every single one.
  • Replace each frame in order as you check it
  • When all frames checked — confirm no queen cells remain. Colony is now hopelessly queenless.
Introducing the queen
  • Retrieve queen cage from indoor storage. Confirm she is alive and moving.
  • Identify the two most central brood frames — nurse bees here are most receptive
  • Place the cage between the two frames, candy end slightly downward — prevents dead attendants from blocking the exit
  • Mesh side facing outward so bees can tend to her through the mesh
  • Remove the fondant tab now and expose the candy immediately. Do not wait until Monday. The bees begin eating through it straight away.
  • Close the hive. Reduce entrance to minimum. Do not open again until Saturday 23rd.
  • Write up the record — time, weather, queen cells destroyed (how many), queen introduced, candy exposed
🤫
Walk Away and Don't Come Back Until Saturday
Most queen losses after introduction come from human interference. The bees are doing something complex and chemical. Leave them entirely undisturbed. The next time you open Carol Klein is Saturday 23rd — not before, not for a quick look.

Saturday 23rd May 2026
First Post-Introduction Check
Look for eggs · check both hives · monitor the June gap
🥚
What You're Looking For
Eggs — not the queen. Eggs confirm she has been released, accepted, and is laying. Tiny, upright, in the base of cells like grains of rice. Good light, sun over your shoulder, loupe if needed. You do not need to see the queen herself.
Carol Klein Hive inspection
  • Suit on, smoker lit. Above 14°C, dry, calm — wait for the right window
  • Remove the empty queen cage. Note whether candy was fully eaten.
  • Look for eggs on the central brood frames — take your time, work methodically
  • Destroy any queen cells present — remove every one still visible
  • Assess frame coverage — are bees occupying more frames than at split?
  • Check stores — feed 1:1 syrup if light, especially if June gap conditions are developing
  • Write up the record — eggs seen or not, frames covered, behaviour, any cells found
Sir David Attenborough Hive inspection
  • Full inspection while suited up — swarm cells, brood pattern, stores
  • A split reduces but does not eliminate swarm pressure. Maintain 7-day inspection cycles through June for both hives.
  • Add a super to Sir David if approaching 7–8 frames covered solidly
  • Confirm grass plug has gone — should have been pushed out within 48 hours of split day
🔍
If No Eggs Seen
Don't panic. The supplier notes it can take 2–3 weeks for a caged queen to return to full lay after introduction stress. Wait another full week before reassessing. Only after 3 weeks with no eggs should you consider failure and contact Black Mountain Honey.
🌧️
The June Gap — East Ayrshire
Three models flagged a potential late-May/early-June nectar dearth between spring blossom (sycamore, hawthorn) and summer flow (clover, willowherb). In Scotland this transition can be abrupt. A split colony with a smaller foraging force and larvae to feed is vulnerable. Monitor stores in both hives weekly and be ready to feed 1:1 syrup to Carol Klein if foraging activity drops.
